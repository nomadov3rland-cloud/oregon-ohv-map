# Oregon OHV Map — setup on your phone

Your own offline trail map. No account, no subscription, nothing expires.
Once it's set up, it works with zero signal — that's the whole point.

**The app:** https://nomadov3rland-cloud.github.io/oregon-ohv-map/
(If this link ever moves to your travweber67 account, the app and your
downloaded maps move with it — nothing else changes.)

## Set it up (about 10 minutes, on Wi-Fi)

1. Open the link above in Chrome on your phone.
2. Tap the **⋮ menu** (top right) → **Add to Home screen** (or "Install app").
   Now it lives on your home screen like a regular app, named **OR OHV Map**.
3. Open it from the home screen. You'll see the map of Oregon.
4. Tap the **📦 Packs** button (bottom right — it has a red dot until the
   Oregon pack is on your phone).
5. Tap **Download** on **All of Oregon** (164 MB). Wait for it to finish —
   stay on Wi-Fi. This is the big one: the whole state's roads, towns,
   rivers, land-ownership colors, forest names and boundaries, the state
   border, every trail and camp in the pack, hillshade and contour lines.
6. Download **Tillamook, Clatsop & Columbia County** too (128 MB) — that's
   your home riding country in close-up detail: Tillamook State Forest
   (Browns Camp, Jordan Creek, Diamond Mill, Rogers Camp), Clatsop State
   Forest / Nicolai Mountain, Mt. Baber, Columbia County.
7. Optional, grab them whenever: **Mt. Hood** (19 MB) and
   **Central Oregon — Bend, Santiam & Ochoco** (64 MB).

All four together: **375 MB** on your phone. Done — you never have to
download them again.

**When a pack gets better:** if a pack card ever says **⬆ Updated version
available**, tap **Update**. It only downloads the files that actually
changed (usually a few MB), not the whole pack again.

## Using it in the woods

- **Blue dot** = you. The **🎯 button** (right side) cycles: tap once to
  follow yourself, tap again and the map turns with your direction
  (heading-up), tap a third time to turn follow off.
- **⏺ Record** = breadcrumb track. Tap to start, ride, tap to stop. The
  track saves on your phone. Find saved tracks in the **☰ menu** — you can
  show them on the map, or export/share them as GPX files.
- **🧭 Route** = tap points on the map to measure a route. It draws
  straight lines between your points and adds up the miles. It's a
  measuring tool, NOT turn-by-turn directions — it says so right on screen.
- **Tap things**: tap a **trail** and its whole route lights up yellow —
  every segment with that trail's name — with a card showing what it's open
  to and its season. Tap a **campsite/staging dot** for its name and details.
  Tap **colored land** to see who owns it and the camping rule of thumb.
- **☰ menu** = layer switches (land colors, trails, camps, hillshade,
  contour lines) and the plain-language legends.
- **🏕️ Camping view** (in the ☰ menu): flips the land colors to a simple
  three-way read — **strong green = camp here** (National Forest + BLM,
  dispersed camping generally allowed), **amber = check the rules**
  (state land, parks, refuges, local land), **flat gray = no camping**
  (tribal land; private land stays uncolored). Flip it off to go back to
  the normal ownership colors.
- **Forest names are printed right on the map** — Tillamook State Forest,
  Willamette National Forest, the BLM districts and so on — with a dark
  outline around each forest, so you can see where one ends and the next
  begins. The **Oregon state border** is a bold dark line at every zoom,
  and county lines show faintly when you zoom in.

## What the land colors mean

- **Green — National Forest** and **tan — BLM**: dispersed camping is
  generally allowed. These are your camp-anywhere colors.
- **Blue — state land** (state forests like Tillamook/Clatsop): camping in
  designated campgrounds and posted dispersed areas.
- **Other muted colors**: other public land (parks, refuges, tribal, local)
  — rules vary, the tap card tells you.
- **No color = private land.**

Trail colors: **orange = MVUM** (the Forest Service's official motorized
map — the legal word on what's open), **green = state forest OHV trails**,
**purple dashed = Forest Service trail inventory**.

## What's covered offline (tested with the network off)

- **Whole state of Oregon**: roads, towns, labels, rivers, land colors,
  trails and camps at overview-to-mid zooms (map detail to zoom 12,
  hillshade everywhere, contour lines every 100 m).
- **Close-up detail (to zoom 15, street/trail level)** in three areas:
  the north coast range (Columbia/Clatsop/Tillamook), Mt. Hood, and
  Central Oregon (Santiam Pass–Bend–Ochoco), with fine 20 m contours.
- Outside those three close-up areas you still get the statewide map —
  just not building-level detail when you zoom all the way in.

## Honest limits

- No offline search and no turn-by-turn directions — by design. It's a
  map, a compass and a track recorder, not a navigator.
- Trail data is only as current as the agencies' published data
  (USFS MVUM + trail inventory, Oregon Dept. of Forestry). If a gate or a
  ranger says a trail is closed, believe the gate, not the app.
- The MVUM layer is the legal reference for motorized use on National
  Forest land; always check the current MVUM for the forest you're in.

## If something looks wrong

Delete a pack and re-download it from the 📦 screen, or remove the home
screen icon and re-add it from the link — your downloaded maps survive
that. Tracks are stored in the app; exporting them as GPX is the backup.
